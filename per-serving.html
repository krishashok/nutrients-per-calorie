The method

The 100 kcal score, scaled to the plate

Each food's per-100 kcal score is multiplied by the calories in a practical serving, then the whole chart is rescaled so the best serving, a piece of mathi, reads 100. Protein is counted the same way, in grams per serving. Mathi's serving is the same under both switches, so scores are comparable across them.

The switch

Two sizes of sabzi

Small serving uses the sizes that sized the dots on the per-100 kcal chart: roughly 100 g of raw vegetable or greens. Large serving is a generous sabzi portion cooked down: about 200 g of raw vegetable, 250 g of raw palak or amaranth, 120 g of methi. Meat, fish, dals, dairy and fruit do not change.

The catch

Greens are still a floor

Palak and amaranth hit the cap on several nutrients at 100 kcal, so their true per-100 kcal score is higher than 330. Scaling a capped number down to a small serving shortchanges them under both switches. Their dots are a floor, not a verdict.

Eat like a human
Half the gap is the size of the serving.

At a small serving, animal foods own the top of the chart. Give the greens a large serving and palak sits level with lean goat, above eggs and liver. Mathi leads either way. The plate still wants both.
